Onboarding & the Plan Picker

What a new account sees before using the app: three short steps ending in a plan. Step 3 is shown in two competing styles so you can pick one. Every mockup is clickable, and every number comes from the formulas we agreed on.

Example person: male, born 1999, 180 cm, 85 kg, moderately active Maintenance: ≈ 2,860 kcal/day "Today": 16 Sep 2026

01The flow at a glance

A brand-new account is sent here right after logging in, and can't reach the rest of the app until a plan is picked. The same flow runs again from Settings when you change your plan.

  1. About youSex, birth year, height, weight and activity level: exactly what Mifflin-St Jeor and the activity factor need, nothing more.
  2. Your goalLose, maintain or gain. Lose and gain ask for a goal weight; maintain goes straight on.
  3. Pick a planMaintenance is shown with a "how we got this" explanation, then the plan choice, then an escape hatch to type your own calories and protein.

06Your own numbers (both options)

The same panel sits under either option. The goal date is recalculated from your calories with 7,700 kcal per kg; if the numbers can never reach the goal, the date is replaced by the warning.

Losing, 2,400 kcal: reachable.
Losing, 3,000 kcal (above maintenance): impossible.

07A or B?

How the two plan styles compare for this app and these two users.

QuestionA · three cardsB · slider
DecidingCompare all options at a glance, one tapSee one option; exploring means dragging
Goal datesAll three dates visible together, the clearest "faster vs. easier" trade-offOne date that changes as you drag
FlexibilityFixed paces; in-between values via "Set my own"Any pace in 0.05 kg steps
On a phoneBig tap targetsSmall drags are fiddly with a thumb
MaintainA single card, consistent with the othersSlider disappears; screen looks different per goal
Build effortSimple list of buttonsSlider + live recalculation + accessible labels
My recommendation: Option A. It shows the real trade-off (how fast vs. how hard) in one glance, is easier to use one-handed, and "Set my own" already covers anyone who wants a value between the fixed paces, so the slider's extra freedom isn't needed.
