Part 3 · new

The clever trick: how it finds the box

To draw the green box, the app needs to know exactly where the barcode is. The barcode reader doesn't tell it much, so the app works the rest out itself.

😬 The problem

The reader (ZXing) only gives back two dots: the left and right ends of the line it read across the stripes (the orange line below). That tells you how wide the barcode is, but not how tall it is or whether it's tilted.

💡 The trick

Draw more lines above and below the orange one and count how many times each crosses between black and white. Lots of switches = still on the stripes. Hardly any = you've gone past the top or bottom of the barcode.

5449000000996

line the reader used test line "still on the stripes"

Without the tilt check 😕

The reader's line always runs straight across the picture, so the box would come out straight and too big.

With the tilt check 🙂

The edges of the stripes all point the same way. The app measures that direction and turns the box to match. This was the second commit.

📐 Last step: fit it to the screen

The camera picture is bigger than the phone screen and gets cropped to fit. The box is scaled and shifted the same way, so it lands exactly on the barcode you see.

🛟 Plan B

If the picture is too blurry to measure, the frame just stays where it is and still shows the number. A failed measurement never stops the scan.

Part 5

Questions your friend might ask

Tap a question to see a short answer.

Does the animation make you wait longer?

No. The product search starts the instant the barcode is read, and the animation plays while the search is running. By the time the scanner closes (0.9 s later), the answer is often already there.

Why does the picture freeze?

The app draws the green box using the snapshot that was read. If the live video kept moving, the barcode would drift away from the box. So that exact snapshot is placed over the video and the box lands on it. A faint white flash, like a camera shutter, hides the switch.

What if the barcode is sideways or upside down?

If a snapshot doesn't read upright, the reader tries it turned 90°. The box is also turned as little as possible: a sideways code gets an upright box with its width and height swapped, so the frame never spins around.

Where does the food info come from?

First from products you've added before (saved to your account, instant). Then from Open Food Facts, a free public food database. If neither knows it, you get a form to add it, and you can photograph the nutrition label to fill it in. Next time it's one scan.

What if the camera is blocked, missing or busy?

A full-screen message explains what happened in plain words ("The camera is blocked", "No camera found", "The camera is busy"). It always offers Type the number instead, plus Try the camera again when that could help.

Will it read any barcode or QR code?

No, only supermarket barcode types (EAN, UPC and a couple of others), and only numbers with 7 to 14 digits. Everything else is ignored, and scanning just carries on.

What about people who get dizzy from animations?

If the phone is set to "reduce motion", nothing flies around. The green box and the number simply appear on the code.

If your friend is a programmer: where it lives

barcode-reader.tsSets up ZXing: which barcode types to look for, and a fix for ZXing's own "turn the picture" feature, which crashed. It also remembers the last snapshot so the screen can freeze on it.

Part 6

Say it in 20 seconds

  1. The camera takes about five snapshots a second, and a barcode library looks for the stripes, upright or sideways.
  2. When one works, the phone buzzes, the screen freezes on that snapshot, and the product search starts right away.
  3. The frame jumps onto the barcode and closes into a green box with the number under it. The app finds the box by sliding lines up and down until they run off the stripes, and tilts it to match.
  4. Under a second later you see the food and its calories. If the camera doesn't work, you just type the number.
