How the barcode scanner works

You point your phone at a food package, the app reads the barcode, finds the product and shows its calories. This page walks through how that happens, with most of the space given to the new look of the scanner.

Redesign: 5 Oct 2026, two commits Main files: BarcodeScanner.tsx · barcode-outline.ts · barcode-reader.ts

01The big picture

Five steps from opening the camera to seeing the product. Only the middle one, the lock-on, is new.

02How it reads a barcode

The reading itself didn't change in the redesign. It uses ZXing, a free, open-source barcode library.

  1. Open the back camera The browser asks for permission. This only works on a secure (HTTPS) page.
  2. Grab a still picture every 0.18 seconds Each snapshot from the video goes to ZXing. Most snapshots contain no readable code. That's normal, and it simply tries the next one.
  3. Look for the stripe pattern ZXing only looks for supermarket formats (EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC, ITF, Code 128). If it finds nothing, it tries the picture turned 90°, which is why sideways codes work too.
  4. Check the number Only results with 7–14 digits count. Anything else is ignored and scanning carries on.
ZXing's own "turn the picture" feature crashed and silently froze the camera. The app replaces that one piece with its own version that draws a rotated copy of the frame (barcode-reader.ts).

04The lock-on moment, step by step New

Everything below happens in under a second, right after ZXing reads a valid number.

  1. Buzz and start the lookup0 ms The phone vibrates briefly and the product search begins in the background straight away, so the animation doesn't add waiting time.
  2. Freeze the picture0 ms The exact camera frame that was read is drawn on top of the live video. The outline then lands on a still image of the barcode, not a moving one. A faint white flash ("photo taken") hides the tiny jump.
  3. Frame flies onto the code0–480 ms The square frame moves, shrinks and turns to fit around the barcode. The background darkens a bit more so the code stands out.
  4. Corners close into an outline380–640 ms The four corners turn mint green and their arms grow until they meet, making a complete box around the code.
  5. The pill becomes the number~60–520 ms "Looking for a barcode" slides under the code, turns green and cross-fades into ✓ and the digits. The bottom text changes to "Got it. Looking it up…".
  6. Hand over900 ms The scanner closes and the product sheet (or a "Looking it up…" sheet, if the search is still running) takes over.
On phones set to "reduce motion", nothing flies: the outline and number simply appear on the code.

05How it knows where the barcode is New

This is the clever part (barcode-outline.ts). ZXing gives back just two points: the two ends of the line it read across the bars. That's enough to know the code's width, but not how tall it is or whether it leans.

1. Start from the read line. The two points give the code's width and direction. The line can cross the bars anywhere, not necessarily in the middle.

2. Find the height. The app tests lines parallel to the read one, moving up and down 2 pixels at a time. As long as a line still crosses lots of black-white edges, it's on the bars. Where the edges stop is where the bars end.

3. Find the lean (second commit). ZXing's read line always runs straight across the picture, even when the code is tilted. So the app checks which way the bar edges point and turns the box to match. It only does this when the edges clearly agree and the tilt is under 30°.

4. Put it on screen. The camera picture is bigger than the phone screen and gets cropped to fit, so the box is scaled and shifted the same way. It's also turned as little as possible: a sideways code gets an upright box with its sides swapped, so the frame never spins a quarter turn.

If any of this can't be worked out (blurry frame, code half off-screen), the outline simply stays where the frame already is and shows the number. Scanning works the same either way.

06Finding the product

Once there's a number, the app asks its own server, which checks two places in order.

WhereWhat happens
1 · Your productsProducts you've added before are saved to your account, so a repeat scan is instant.
2 · Open Food FactsA free public food database. The app takes the name, brand, photo and nutrition per 100 g or ml.
Not foundAn "Add this product" form opens. You can photograph the nutrition label to fill it in. The product is then saved, so next time it's one scan.

08The 30-second version for your friend

"The camera takes about five snapshots a second and a barcode library looks for the stripes, upright or sideways. When it finds a valid number, two things happen at once: the app starts looking the product up, and the screen freezes on that exact frame.

Then comes the new part. The scanning frame jumps onto the barcode and closes into a green outline, tilted to match it, with the number underneath. To get that outline, the app takes the line the reader used and slides parallel lines up and down until they run off the bars. That gives the height, and the direction of the bar edges gives the tilt.

Under a second later you see the product. If the camera doesn't work, you just type the number."
